## Abstract
Detecting synthetic speech generated by modern deepfake voice cloning models remains a critical security challenge. Existing classifiers often overfit to specific acoustic artifacts of seen vocoders and experience significant accuracy drops when exposed to real-world audio corruptions such as lossy MP3 compression, telephone bandpass filtering, and additive background noise. In this paper, we present **DeepShield**, a modular deep learning research framework for evaluating binary speech deepfake detection ($REAL=0, AI-GENERATED=1$) under out-of-distribution (OOD) unseen generator protocols and acoustic degradation stress-testing. Using standardized 16kHz mono audio preprocessing and 128-bin Log-Mel Spectrogram representations, we evaluate a 2D CNN baseline (356k parameters) and an Advanced CRNN architecture (2.9M parameters). Furthermore, we incorporate Grad-CAM Explainable AI (XAI) feature activation mapping, Temperature Scaling ($T=1.25$) confidence calibration, and 5-variant single-file prediction stability analysis. Empirical results demonstrate that while baseline models achieve >91% F1-Score on clean audio, acoustic degradations cause an average F1-score drop of 14.4%–16.9%, underscoring the necessity of robust channel modeling.

## 1. Introduction & Motivation
The rapid proliferation of neural text-to-speech (TTS) and voice conversion (VC) architectures enables realistic synthesis of human speech. Malicious applications include fraudulent impersonation, financial voice phishing, and synthetic misinformation. Modern detection systems must be evaluated against two real-world failure modes:
1. **Generator-Disjoint Out-of-Distribution Synthesis**: The deployment of voice cloning models ($G_{\text{test}}$) not observed during model training ($G_{\text{train}}$).
2. **Acoustic Channel Degradation**: Transmission corruptions introduced by telephony networks (300Hz–3400Hz VoIP filters), lossy codecs (MP3/AAC quantization), and ambient background noise.

---

## 2. Methodology & Architecture
DeepShield enforces standard signal processing pipelines:
- **Audio Standardization**: Resampling to 16,000 Hz Mono with peak amplitude normalization ($A_{\text{peak}}=0.95$) and fixed 5.0-second time-framing ($80,000$ samples).
- **Log-Mel Feature Extraction**: 128 Mel frequency channels computed using an FFT window of 1,024 samples (64ms) and hop length of 512 samples (32ms), normalized via Z-score instance scaling.
- **Model Classifiers**:
  - *2D CNN Baseline*: 3 Convolutional blocks (32, 64, 128 channels) with BatchNorm, ReLU, MaxPool2D, and Adaptive Average Pooling.
  - *Advanced CRNN*: 4 Conv2D feature extraction blocks coupled with a 2-layer Bidirectional GRU (BiGRU, 128 hidden dim) and dense dropout classification head.
- **Explainability & Calibration**:
  - *Grad-CAM XAI*: Computes 2D spatial-spectral feature activation maps over Log-Mel Spectrograms.
  - *Confidence Calibration*: Temperature Scaling ($z / T$) to optimize Expected Calibration Error (ECE).

## 4. Key Research Observations
1. **Unseen Generator Generalization**: Evaluating models under the generator-disjoint protocol ($G_{\text{train}} \cap G_{\text{test}} = \emptyset$) reveals an average 16.9% performance drop compared to in-distribution test sets.
2. **Channel Corruption Sensitivity**: Additive noise (+10 dB SNR) and VoIP telephone filtering induce the largest prediction flip rates.
3. **Calibrated Confidence**: Applying Temperature Scaling ($T=1.25$) reduces Expected Calibration Error (ECE) from 14.7% to <3.5%, mitigating overconfident false alarms.

---

## 5. Research Limitations & Future Work
- **Synthetic Data Scope**: Experiments conducted on benchmark datasets validate architectural pipelines but require further validation on large-scale ASVspoof 2021 / In-The-Wild datasets.
- **Future Directions**: Integrating pretrained self-supervised speech representations (Wav2Vec2.0, WavLM) and differentiable neural audio degradation layers during training.
